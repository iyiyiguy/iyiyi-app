How to use it

Press play to watch liquidation levels build up and get swept as price moves. Scroll your mouse wheel over the chart, drag across it, use the slider, or press ← → to scrub (hold Shift to jump 10 frames). Space plays and pauses. Each frame is one bar of the frame size you pick, from 1 minute to 1 month. Type any coin's ticker or paste a token contract address in the search box. Pick a date to replay that period, or press Now to go live; in live mode the chart updates by itself every few seconds. Load older adds 500 earlier frames. Turn layers on and off from the Layers menu.

Cyan bands are prices where longs would be liquidated (they sit below price). Pink bands are where shorts would be liquidated (above price). The brighter the band, the more money is estimated there. The bars on the right show the levels at the current frame.

What this is (and isn't)

Like every liquidation heatmap, this is an estimate. No exchange publishes where its traders' liquidation prices or stop losses are, so nobody has that data. LiqReplay estimates it the standard way: when open interest rises, new positions are assumed to open near that bar's price, split between longs and shorts using the long/short account ratio and taker buy/sell volume, spread across 5×–100× leverage. Levels are removed when price trades through them (liquidated) or when open interest falls (positions closed). The first part of each replay starts empty and fills in as positions are added.

Data: OKX USDT perpetuals, loaded live from OKX (price, open interest, long/short account ratio, taker volume); contract addresses are matched to coins through DexScreener. Only coins with a perpetual futures market have open interest, so tokens without one can't be mapped. OKX keeps 1,440 periods of open-interest history for each frame size: about 5 days of 1- and 5-minute frames, 15 days of 15-minute, 60 days of 1-hour, 240 days of 4-hour, about 4 years of daily, and all weekly and monthly. 1-minute frames use 5-minute open interest.
